**Introduction**:
	Buy Green allows users to browse through several product categories to see how sustainable each product is and the price of each product. It compares each product to other options to recommend more sustainable and affordable options. There are three parameters used to view different alternatives, which are the green value score, eco score, and the affordability score. The eco score measures how sustainable a product is and how little impact it causes on the environment, while the affordability score compares the price per quantity of each product in comparison to other products from the same category. The green value score is an original score we came up with that takes both the eco score and affordability score into account when displaying alternatives. We coded a slider that allows the user to decide how important the eco score and affordability score are to their decision-making process. For example, the user can put the slider at the point where they want the eco score to be 60% of the value while the affordability score will be 40% of the value. This shows alternatives that take both scores into account, allowing the user to view eco-friendly options within their budget.
	Another feature in the app is the forum feature, which allows users to make posts in order to recommend local businesses that are sustainable. We’ve both been to many local businesses in our community, and most of these businesses are not on big databases such as the ones we used. This is why we decided to add this feature, so that people are more aware of any small or local businesses near them that engage in sustainable practices so that they can receive more support.
	The last core feature of our app is the scanner feature. This feature allows users to scan a product while they’re shopping at a store so that they can see the eco score of the product and view the alternative options as well. This is a lot more convenient for users since they can immediately scan the barcode rather than having to search through the categories.

**Inspiration**:
  We were inspired to create this app by the lack of connection between consumer decisions and environmental impact. When we were in elementary school, we both experienced the effects of pollution in the cities we grew up in, such as smog, deforestation, and other environmental issues. While we were brainstorming app ideas, we realized that our choices as buyers could be causing similar environmental problems in the present for other little kids.
  With our society becoming increasingly focused on convenience and profit, it is easy to ignore the environmental costs of the material goods in our houses. Fast fashion, unsustainable farming practices, and the overproduction of livestock and agriculture are affecting the environment in more ways than most people are aware of. Both of us have seen a lot of these effects in the present, like the unpredictable weather and the impact on air quality. As we learned more about human-centered climate change, we realized the connection between our lives and worldwide environmental issues was much bigger than we thought. 
  We wanted to prove that our actions matter. Throughout our lives, we have experienced the advent of cheaper materials, faster delivery, changing trends, and the normalization of overconsumption. Yet, while it’s easy to see the sticker price of a product, it is much harder to identify its lasting impact on the environment. We believe the constantly changing trends and cultures around us should not have an impact on our beloved planet. However, making responsible choices should not come at the expense of convenience or time either. 
  Ultimately, we created Buy Green out of a shared belief that we are responsible for our planet and the people on it. We wanted to encourage people to account for the environmental costs of products in their daily lives and hope to turn sustainability into a constant consideration. 

**Technical Difficulties**:
While programming our app, we faced a few technical difficulties, mostly in the connections between our various layers and algorithms. We used many tools for the very first time, like Expo Go, and had to learn many new concepts on the run. 
	The very first problem we encountered was calling the Open Food Facts API. While the API had a gold mine of information, we were restricted to only 10 calls per minute, which would make our idea of having 5 alternatives for each product nearly impossible. We resolved this by focusing more on the algorithmic and UI aspects of our app and locally storing a list of 33 products in a JSON file for prototype purposes. 
	In our app, a major feature was the barcode functionality due to the invaluable convenience in the app. However, we faced major roadblocks in implementing this feature in the app. When we first integrated Expo Camera’s CameraView into the app code file, we only saw a black screen when we clicked on the barcode feature. We later found out this was because we needed to request the necessary permissions from the phone in order to properly utilize the barcode and camera feature. After setting this up, we faced issues with matching the barcode with the barcodes in the JSON, as the ones from the files often had a missing 0 at the beginning. To fix this, we changed the matching algorithm to match with a product that had the entire detected barcode in the product barcodes in order to bypass this issue. 
	Another issue we faced was properly calculating the affordability and GreenValue scores so that they reflect a mix of affordability and sustainability. While we were first going to utilize a simple percent difference algorithm to calculate the affordability score, we soon realized that the quantity bought determines the true value. Then, in order to ensure there wasn’t a huge difference in affordability scores at small prices, we utilized a smooth square root formula that determined the score based on price per ounce. 
	These issues, and many others along the way, were frustrating at the moment but helped us learn and further optimize our app. We are grateful for the lessons we learned while making this app. 

**Improvements**:
  Our app is currently a prototype version, so we focused on the major functional elements of our app. However, we did not have time to expand the scope of our app or add more beneficial features. Given the opportunity to make a second version of our app, we would add several of these functionalities to further enhance the user experience of our app. 
  The first major change we would make would be to add more products and categories to our app. Currently, the products we have are helpful to demonstrate the features of our app but might not be useful to someone buying a whole grocery haul. We would like to change that by expanding our product base and making our app universal for popular food products. 
  The second major change that we can make is to add accounts and history to our app. Through this alteration, we can save the most commonly searched products for each user and make recently searched products convenient to reach. This way, we can even create personalized recommendations for each user, allowing them to find sustainable products that they might have overlooked. 
  The last major change that we would make would be to have our data files change from static to dynamic. Currently, our product data must be changed manually or will stay the same. We would like our product information to update to price and information changes in order to stay up-to-date and allow our users to have the most relevant information. 
  These changes would help our app become more convenient, useful, and sustainable in the long run.
